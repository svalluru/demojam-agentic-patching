Demo guide

Before you open the door

  • Run ./demo.sh and open http://127.0.0.1:8788. Confirm the red banner across the top before anyone is watching.
  • Reset to a clean slate between runs: rm -f runs/simulated.json, then reload.
  • Close this drawer with Esc. You can reopen it mid-demo — it does not disturb the run.
  • On a projector, set Text size to Large in the gear panel. It is a browser setting — nothing is sent to the server and nobody else's view changes.
  • Budget 10 minutes for the seven beats below, plus questions. The short version is beats 1, 2, 4 and 6.

The sentence it all hangs off

Patching is not slow because applying a patch is slow. It is slow because deciding which hosts to patch, and getting a person to agree, takes longer than the patch does. This puts an agent on the deciding and keeps the person on the agreeing.

If you say one thing, say that. Everything below is evidence for it.

Run of show

1

The system, before anything runs 30s

DoPoint at the left panel. Nothing has run yet, so every box is grey.
Say“That is the whole system — a supervisor, the agents it drives, and the services behind them. Two of the agents are not built, which is why they are dashed, and they will still be dashed at the end. Everything else lights up as it gets called.”
Lead with this because it is the one claim nobody else can make from a slide: the diagram is drawn from the agents' own event log, not from a drawing of what the flow is supposed to be. Say that out loud — it is the difference between a demo and an animation.
2

Assess the critical CVE 90s

DoPress Assess on the CVE-2024-3094 row. Then stop talking and let the left panel move.
Say“Insights hands over the CVE and the affected hosts. The supervisor wakes the first agent, which goes to the risk model and to the patch history store, both of those in parallel. Every line you just watched light up is a logged call. If one of those boxes had stayed grey, nothing would have asked it.”
The pause is the point. People expect a spinner; what they get is a system describing itself while it works. Do not narrate over it.
3

Read the plan like a reviewer would 90s

DoWalk the plan: the score per host, that host's own patch history, the maintenance window, the verdict.
Say“This is what a human approves against, so it has to carry everything that decision needs. All five hosts came back above the review threshold, so the verdict is manual review.”
DoPoint at the dispatch button before you press it. It reads Send 5 for approval, in black.
Say“The button is labelled from the verdict. It never says patch when it is not about to patch anything.”
4

The gate — the beat that sells it 2 min

DoSend the five. Approve one host. Watch its bar fill. Leave the others sitting at the gate.
Say“Every host got its own workflow run and every one of them stopped. Nothing moves until somebody approves it. That bar is the fraction of workflow steps that have reported finished — it is not a timer. If the run stalls, the bar stalls.”
Say the bar line to an ops audience specifically. They have all been lied to by a progress bar, and volunteering that yours is measured buys more credibility than the rest of the demo put together.
5

The other path: no gate at all 60s

DoGo back to the list and assess CVE-2024-1086.
Say“Lower severity, both hosts under the threshold, so there is no approval gate anywhere downstream. Notice the button went red and now says Patch 2 hosts — because that press is the only human check these hosts get. Nothing stops to ask again.”
Shows the routing is a real decision rather than a fixed script, and that the console changes its own language depending on the consequence. Also worth pointing at: the RAG store stays grey on this one, because no host needed review so nothing ever asked it.
6

What it saved 2 min

DoPress Calculate ROI. Read the banner at the top out loud before you touch a number.
Say“The measured half is this run's own clock. The manual half is assumed — it has to be, because the manual run never happened. So the first card is a slider for every assumption behind the comparison. If you think fifteen minutes of change prep per host is generous, drag it down now and the whole page follows.”
DoHand the laptop over and let them move a slider. If you do one interactive thing all demo, do this one.
Say“Further down it tells you how far those assumptions have to move before the conclusion flips, and the payback card says outright that the platform cost is the weakest number on the page.”
A savings page nobody can argue with is a page nobody believes. Handing over the sliders converts the most sceptical person in the room into the one operating it.
7

Close on the banner 20s

DoPoint back at the red bar at the top of the page.
Say“Everything you just saw ran with no cluster attached. No host was touched and those workflow runs exist nowhere. The agent logic, the routing, the gate and the plan are the real ones — the data under them is stored, and the console says so on every screen rather than letting you find out later.”
Ending on the limitation is not a weak close. It is the reason they will believe the rest of it, and it pre-empts the question that was coming anyway.

If they ask

“Is any of this real?” The agents, the control flow, the approval gate, the thresholds and the routing rules are. The CVEs, hosts, scores and history are stored defaults, and the same console runs against real backends with ./demo.sh --live.
“What happens when one of your backends is down?” Reads degrade, writes fail closed. A step that could not reach its service falls back to a stored default, and then the plan names the step, the service and the fix, and that box turns red in the diagram. A patch is never assumed — that asymmetry is deliberate, because a missing risk score can be estimated and a patch cannot.
“Can it patch without a human?” Yes, below the threshold, and the threshold is a setting — the gear at the top right. Show them. Being able to move the line between supervised and unattended in front of them is more convincing than any claim about where it sits.
“Where does the patch history come from?” A vector store — Milvus with Llama Stack in front of it — queried per host, and only for hosts that need review. The model is XGBoost, served on OpenShift.
“How do we know the agent did what you say it did?” The trace under the diagram is every hop, in order, with the seconds between them, and the API carries the same list. Scroll it. It is the same source the diagram draws from, so the two cannot disagree.
“Can we change the thresholds and the costs?” Gear icon, every tunable the process reads, grouped, with its default shown until something overrides it. In-memory and lost on restart; .env is the durable place.

Do not say

  • Do not call the ROI figures a measured saving. Half of that comparison is assumed and the page says so. Claiming otherwise is the one thing on here that would be caught later rather than in the room.
  • Do not claim the risk score discriminates between hosts yet. Three of its four inputs describe the CVE rather than the host, so hosts of one CVE score almost alike. It needs tuning, and saying so costs you nothing.
  • Do not skip the banner to make it look live. If someone spots it after you have implied otherwise, you lose the whole demo and not just that slide.
  • Do not run --live in front of a customer without checking first. The AAP job templates the workflow calls do not exist yet, so an approved run against a real Ansible Orchestrator fails part way through. The offline path is unaffected — demo on that.
  • Do not present agents 3 and 4 as coming soon with a date. They are not started. The diagram already shows them dashed; let it.

Between demos

Delete the run store and reload — rm -f runs/simulated.json — otherwise the ROI page's lifetime card carries your last rehearsal into the next room. Everything else is in memory and resets when you restart ./demo.sh.
